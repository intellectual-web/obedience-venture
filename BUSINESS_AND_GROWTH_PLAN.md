# Obedience Ventures — Business Development and Growth Plan
**Brand promise:** Tested and Trusted  
**Base:** Kaduna State, Nigeria  
**Planning principle:** Start with verifiable capability; expand when demand, working capital, suppliers and compliance are ready.

## 1. Business position and offer
Position Obedience Ventures as an agriculture-focused sourcing, trading and relationship-building venture. In the early stage, avoid presenting every listed service as fully operational. State clearly what is available now, what is offered through a verified partner, and what remains a future goal.

**Suggested initial offer**
- Source and supply one or two farm-product categories that can be fulfilled consistently.
- Connect local producers with interested buyers after checking product quality, quantity, location, price and delivery terms.
- Provide basic agribusiness support only within demonstrated competence; refer specialist veterinary, legal, export or technical work to qualified professionals.

## 2. First 30 days: establish the basics
1. Choose a primary customer group: households/retailers, restaurants, livestock keepers, farmers, processors, or bulk buyers. Start with one or two.
2. Choose no more than two initial product categories based on local access, storage needs, perishability, working capital and likely demand.
3. Build a supplier register: name, location, products, capacity, seasonal availability, indicative prices, quality standards, lead time and references.
4. Build a buyer register: buyer type, required product/specification, quantity, delivery location, frequency, payment terms and contact permission.
5. Prepare a written quotation template, order confirmation, delivery note, receipt/invoice and complaint/returns process.
6. Set up a separate record of business income, costs, transport, packaging, spoilage, credit and net margin.
7. Confirm applicable registration, tax, local permits and sector requirements with the appropriate Nigerian authorities or a qualified adviser before taking regulated or export orders.

## 3. Days 31–60: test demand before investing heavily
- Interview at least 10 potential buyers and 10 potential suppliers.
- Request current quotes from several suppliers for the same specification.
- Complete a small pilot transaction only after confirming the buyer, stock, quality, transport, payment and margin.
- Record every cost: purchase price, transport, loading, packaging, market fees, losses, payment charges and time.
- Do not promise stock that is not confirmed. Avoid buying large perishable quantities based only on verbal interest.
- Ask satisfied customers for permission to use a genuine testimonial; never invent reviews.

## 4. Days 61–90: improve repeat business
- Focus on the products and customer types that produced reliable delivery and positive contribution after costs.
- Offer repeat-order reminders and clear quotations.
- Build a simple WhatsApp Business catalogue with current, accurate product information.
- Publish short educational updates and actual product photos, with permission where people or farms are identifiable.
- Review weekly: enquiries, quotations, conversion rate, repeat orders, gross margin, delivery reliability, complaints and cash outstanding.

## 5. Website and marketing
- Keep the green-and-gold logo and the “TESTED AND TRUSTED” tagline consistent across the website, quotations, WhatsApp Business and business documents.
- Use clear calls to action: “Ask about availability”, “Request a quotation” and “Discuss a supply partnership”.
- Add genuine photos, product specifications, service area and clear availability status as the business develops.
- Do not publish false stock, certifications, customer testimonials, export capability, office addresses or international partnerships.
- Collect only information needed to respond to enquiries, and keep customer details secure.
- Track basic website results using privacy-conscious analytics if desired, and measure enquiries that turn into completed orders.

## 6. Practical customer acquisition
- Visit or call local farmers, farmer groups, produce traders, retailers, restaurants, food processors and livestock businesses.
- Introduce the business with a short, specific offer—not a promise to supply everything.
- Use a simple message: “Obedience Ventures is building reliable sourcing connections for [specific product] in [area]. What specification and quantity do you usually need? We will confirm availability and send a quotation where we can fulfil it.”
- Ask existing contacts for referrals only after a successful transaction.
- Build trust through punctual updates, accurate weights/specifications, written terms, receipts and prompt resolution of problems.

## 7. International opportunities — readiness pathway
International trade should be a staged goal, not a claim of current capability unless the business already has the required supply chain and documentation.

**Stage A: research**
- Choose one product and one target market.
- Learn the target country's import rules, quality specifications, labelling, packaging, traceability, phytosanitary/veterinary requirements and buyer expectations.
- Check whether the product is commercially viable after aggregation, grading, packaging, storage, inland transport, freight, insurance, inspection, finance and potential rejection costs.

**Stage B: verify**
- Speak to potential buyers and obtain written specifications and indicative demand.
- Verify buyer identity and references independently.
- Confirm supplier capacity and product quality through samples or inspections.
- Consult the relevant Nigerian export-promotion, customs, standards and agricultural/quarantine authorities or a qualified export professional about current requirements. Requirements vary by product and destination.
- For plant products, investigate applicable phytosanitary documentation; for animals or animal products, investigate veterinary health, disease-control, processing and destination-specific restrictions. Do not assume a product is exportable simply because a buyer requests it.

**Stage C: pilot**
- Start with a low-risk, compliant trial shipment only when requirements, documentation, payment security, logistics and margins are clear.
- Use a written contract defining specification, inspection, delivery terms, rejection procedure, payment milestones and dispute resolution.
- Do not pay suspicious “registration”, “release” or “guaranteed buyer” fees without independently verifying the organisation and service.

## 8. Risk controls
- **Price volatility:** short quote validity and written confirmation before purchase.
- **Spoilage/quality:** clear specifications, inspection, appropriate storage and handling.
- **Payment default:** buyer verification, deposits or safer payment terms appropriate to the transaction.
- **Supplier failure:** backup suppliers and confirmed stock before accepting orders.
- **Fraud:** independently verify identities, bank details and trade documents; beware pressure and advance-fee schemes.
- **Regulatory risk:** verify current requirements with official agencies or qualified advisers before committing.
- **Cash flow:** do not use money needed for personal essentials to fund speculative stock.

## 9. Company profile draft
Obedience Ventures is an agriculture-focused business based in Kaduna State, Nigeria. The venture is developing opportunities in agricultural sourcing, livestock and farm-produce trade, and connections between producers and buyers. Guided by the promise “Tested and Trusted,” Obedience Ventures aims to build dependable relationships through clear communication, verified availability and responsible growth. Product and service availability is confirmed directly for each enquiry.
